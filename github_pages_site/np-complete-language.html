Section 6

An NPR Complete Language

Keming Meng · 15-455 Project Writeup

We are now ready to present an NPR complete language for our BSS machines.

First, define

R[x1, . . . , xn]

to be the real polynomials with the variables x1, . . . , xn.

Definition 11. The language semi-algebraic feasibility, called

SA − FEAS = {(ϕ1, . . . , ϕn) : ϕj = (fj1, . . . , fjm, gj1, . . . , gjk), fji, gjℓ ∈ R[x1, . . . , xp]}

where there is some x such that

ϕ(x) = fj1 > 0 ∨ . . . ∨ fjm > 0 ∨ gj1 = 0 ∨ . . . ∨ gjk = 0

is true for each 1 ≤ j ≤ n. (This definition is adapted/quoted/paraphrased from the book ”Complexity and Real Computation” which referenced in the acknowledgments.)

Remark 3. One might wonder how we encode instances of SA − FEAS. The primary worry is in encoding the polynomials. To do so, we can first note that for any given polynomial with a certain degree over a certain number of variables, we can impart a total order on the monomials. Using such a total order, we can then for each polynomial give its degree, the number of variables it has, and the coefficients on its monomials one by one.

Remark 4. The semi-algebraic system obtained from the register equations in the previous section can be turned into the form of input for SA − FEAS in polynomial time.

Now, we claim that SA − FEAS is a an NPR complete language.

The fact that SA − FEAS is in NP is clear by the fact that as a proof string, we can simply provide a value of x that witnesses each of the φj being satisfied.

We now prove the main theorem that remains.

Theorem 2. SA − FEAS is NPR hard.

Proof. To show the above, we reduce any NPR problem to SA − FEAS via a mapping reduction. First, fix a language L ∈ NPR. We know then that x ∈ L iff there is a polynomial-time verifier V, and a polynomial p such that there is some y with |y| ≤ p(|x|), such that V (x, y) accepts. Let T = q(|x|) bound the time complexity of V. Now, consider the time-q(|x|) register equations for V with input (x, y) and output 1, with x fixed from before. Using the main result of the previous section, these register equations can be reduced to a semi-algebraic system with polynomial size in |x|, since the time is polynomial in |x|, the size of y is at most polynomial in |x|, and the degree of the polynomials in the semi-algebraic system is constant. Call this semi-algebraic system ϕT (x, y, z). Then, note that the map sending x → ϕT (x, y, z) with the value of x plugged in and with ϕT (x, y, z) in the form of SA − FEAS is polynomial time computable as we discussed previously, and is a mapping reduction reducing L to SA − FEAS, as x ∈ L iff there is some y within the polynomial bounds such that the register equations are satisfied in time T, which occurs iff ϕT (x, y, z) is feasible, and so we are done.
