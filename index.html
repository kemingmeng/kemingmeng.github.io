Introduction

Keming Meng · 15-455 Project Writeup

Thus far into the course, the algorithms and models of computation that we have seen have been fundamentally discrete problems. Each of the models of computation that we have seen use bits as the primary way of representing inputs.

It is natural to wonder then, what computation looks like when we instead focus on non-bit centered models of computation. In particular, numerical-algorithms to solve problems like computing solutions to differential equations are a large class of algorithms that get bogged down in the standard Turing Machine representation of computation.

Moreover, thus far we have primarily studied languages over finite length strings over finite size alphabets. It is then natural to wonder what decidability and difficulty of computation looks like for sets over the real numbers and complex numbers. For example, the Mandelbrot set is a fractal shape generated by looking at the set of complex numbers satisfying some iteration conditions. Then, what can we say about the computation complexity of this set, if it is even computable?

One way to get by this, is by studying a model of computation with more power, that allows for access to real numbers and comparisons with real numbers without extra cost. To do so, we first define such machines, specifically over \(\mathbb R\), however the work also generalizes to not only other fields, but also to rings. We then look at decidability, and work up to our first large result, that being the existence of a set that is undecidable.

Then, we look at how to define cost in this model of computation, and study the complexity classes \(P\) and \(NP\) along with completeness and reductions, ultimately proving a Cook-Levin type result for these real number machines.

As a preface, the content in this project is based on the work in the book ”Complexity and Real Computation” by Lenore Blum, Felipe Cucker, Michael Shub, and Steve Smale
